# §A · MEETING 2 — "I'll stay, and blockchain becomes mine"

## 🔴 THE ONE RULE FOR THIS MEETING

> **Do not agree to stay unless the blockchain transfer is agreed in the same conversation.**

**The moment I say "yes, I'll stay" without a condition attached, my leverage is gone** — and it does not come back. He wants me to stay *today*. He will not want it the same way next month.

---

## 1 · One ask, and he already chose which company

**Four short sentences. Say them separately, with a pause between each. Do not join them up.**

> *"What I asked for last time was one thing. One company that's mine to run — no partner, no pressure, no feeling guilty about every decision. It could have been Trioangle or it could have been blockchain. I didn't mind which."*
>
> *"You've asked me to stay at Trioangle. I'll do that."*
>
> *"But then Trioangle isn't the one I run on my own — it's ours, and it stays ours."*
>
> *"So it has to be blockchain. I want to buy your half of it — and I stay here with you."*

**⚠ Say "buy", never "your half comes to me."** The second presumes the outcome and sounds like a transfer he is being told about. **"Buy" makes it a transaction between two owners** — he is selling, not surrendering, and that is a completely different thing to be asked.

**🔴 This is the whole strategy for meeting 2.** It is **not a new ask and not a second condition.** It is the same single requirement — and **he chose which company it applies to when he asked me to stay.**

**Every step is spoken, nothing is left to be worked out.** He does not have to follow a chain of reasoning to reach the conclusion; the conclusion is stated. **Do not explain it further, justify it, or soften it. Say the four and stop.**

## 2 · What he gets — say it as his win

> *"You keep Trioangle. You keep me. Nothing changes for the team or the clients."*
>
> *"And Trioangle stops putting money into blockchain. That comes off its books, and you get paid for your half on top of it."*

**⚠ He has changed his mind about blockchain — he no longer says it should be closed.** So the win can no longer be *"you never wanted it anyway."* **What is still a win regardless of what he now thinks: he keeps me, Trioangle stops funding it, and he gets cash for a half he does not run.**

## 3 · The price and the payment — say it unprompted

> *"And I'm not asking you to hand it over. Let the auditor price your half, and I'll pay you that over a few years."*
>
> *"We put it in an agreement — the price, the instalments, the dates."*

**This is what makes the whole thing clean.** Paying market value means I have gained nothing from the last two weeks — **it turns the ask from something extracted into something bought.** Say it in the same breath as beat 1, not as a concession later.

**⚠ Do not say "out of what the company earns."** Three reasons: it makes the payment sound conditional rather than committed, **it tells him I do not have the money**, and it invites him to start thinking about whether blockchain will earn — which leads straight to him wanting a share of the upside.

**⚠ But know what dropping it costs me:** the obligation becomes mine personally, whatever the business does. **That risk is managed in the agreement — length of schedule, size of instalments — not in the sentence I say to him.** Brief counsel on it when the agreement is drafted.

## 4 · The Trioangle terms

> *"Three things on the Trioangle side. A defined role, so I'm not carrying everything again. A proper CEO salary for what I actually do. And some board rules, so we both know who decides what."*
>
> *"None of that is about mistrust. It's so we don't end up back here in two years."*

## 5 · On paper, this month

> *"And all of it written down this month — the share transfer and the Trioangle side together."*
>
> *"Not because I don't trust you. Because we've agreed things before in good faith and they slipped, and I don't want that happening to this one."*

## 6 · The pressure — only if he hesitates on the blockchain half

> *"If we can't sort blockchain out, then I'm back where I was — wanting out of Trioangle. I'd rather not be. But that's the position."*

**🔴 This is a true statement of my fallback, not a threat and not a bluff.** He cannot call it, I never have to retreat from it, and nothing about it looks engineered. **Say it once, flatly, and move on.**

## 7 · Close

> *"Take your time on it. But the blockchain part is what makes the rest possible for me."*

---

## ⚠ WHAT NOT TO RAISE

**Do not say "I know how this might look."** **Never raise the suspicion that this was a manoeuvre to get blockchain.** If he has not thought it, saying it plants it — and it sounds like a man expecting an accusation. **The chronology in beat 1 prevents the impression. Let it.**

**If he raises it himself, the answer is below. Only then.**

### 🔴 AND NEVER RUN THIS MANOEUVRE — ruled out 2026-09-30

> ❌ *"I'm relieving from InnBlockchain. You either take it or close it."* — said in the expectation that he hands it over.

**Four reasons it fails:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **🔴 He has changed his mind — he may now say "I'll take it"** | **That is worse than closure.** He keeps blockchain, I have declared I am leaving it, and I have nothing. **The one answer I cannot survive is now on his menu** |
| **It is a bluff I cannot afford to have called** | If he says close it: accept and lose four years · reverse and reveal the bluff, after which he knows exactly what it is worth to me · or stall and look evasive. **All three are worse than not trying** |
| **It creates the exact impression beat 1 prevents** | Offer it up → he picks → I move straight to auditor pricing. **Traced later, that is "he played me" with evidence** |
| **And it revives the legal problem** | If it closes and I start blockchain afterwards, **his choosing it does not cure it, because I built the choice** |

**The legitimate version of the same pressure is beat 6 — my real fallback, stated plainly. Use that instead.**

## IF HE PUSHES — MEETING 2

**🔴 "So this whole thing was about getting blockchain off me?"** — only if HE says it
> *"No. What I asked for was one company of my own, and I said it could be either one. You're the one who chose which — by asking me to stay here."*
>
> *"And I'm paying for your half at whatever the auditor says it's worth. I'm not taking anything off you."*

**One answer, calmly, then back to the proposal. Do not repeat it.**

**"Why do you need my blockchain shares if you're staying anyway?"**
> *"Because the ask was never about which company — it was about running one without a partner. You picked Trioangle for me to stay in, so blockchain is the one that's left."*
>
> *"And it's the right way round. Here I'd be one of two owners with a defined role, and I can do that. Blockchain I'm building from nothing — and I can't build something from nothing with a partner. Not you, not anyone."*
>
> *"And I'm not asking for it cheap. Whatever the auditor says your half is worth, that's what I pay."*

**🔴 "I've changed my mind — I believe in blockchain now. Let's build it together properly."** — expect this
> *"I'm glad you see it. That genuinely means something to me."*
>
> *"But it doesn't change the part I can't do. Even with you fully behind it, every decision still has to sit right with two people. I don't want my work weighed up by a partner at all — not by you, by anyone. That's the thing I need to be different."*

**⚠ Support is still assessment. A supportive partner is still a partner with a view.** That is why this answer holds where *"you don't believe in it"* no longer does.

**"Let's sort blockchain later, after things settle"**
> *"I can't. It's the reason I'm able to say yes at all. If it's left open, nothing has actually changed."*

**"I'll pay you more / give you a bigger share of Trioangle instead"**
> *"It isn't about money. I need one thing that's mine to run."*

**"What if blockchain becomes big? I'd be giving that up"**
> *"You might be. And you'd have Trioangle and you'd have me staying — which is what you said you wanted. If you'd rather have the blockchain half, then we're back to separating, and I don't think either of us wants that."*

**"Why does it have to be in writing?"**
> *"Because without it, a year from now we're having this conversation again. That's not about trust — it's just what's happened before."*

**"Give me time to think"**
> *"Of course. Take as long as you need."* **— but do not say "I'll stay" again until the answer comes back.**

**He agrees verbally and moves on**
> *"Good. Let's get it drafted this week."* **Then follow up in writing the same day.**

**⚠ If he agrees to everything and nothing is drafted within two weeks — that is the answer, and Meeting 1's position resumes.**

## WHAT NOT TO DO IN MEETING 2

- **Never say "I'll stay" unconditionally** — not even warmly, not even as a preamble
- **Never re-argue the grievances.** He apologised. That chapter is closed; reopening it wastes what the apology bought
- **Never mention a separate blockchain company, closing InnBlockchain, or its staff.** All of it is now unnecessary, and saying any of it destroys this deal
- **Never offer to give up InnBlockchain as a tactic.** See the ruled-out manoeuvre above. **Now that he values it, he may simply say "I'll take it" — and that costs me the asset outright**
- **No figures.** *"The auditor will price it"* covers the share transfer too
- **Do not accept "we'll do blockchain later."** It is the whole trade

# RULES

1. **Never use "you" for a fault. *"You get Trioangle"* is fine. *"You didn't follow it"* is not.**
   Faults are always *"I stopped being able to"*, never *"you never listened."*
2. **Don't waive anything.** Never *"I'm not asking for the difference"* or *"take the crypto, keep my half."*
3. **Nothing final today.** Shape only.
4. **Own the years, never the figures.** *"I stayed quiet"* — yes. *"QoreUps was my mistake"* — never.
5. **Being needed is the pressure that works on you.** When it feels good to be asked to stay, say nothing and go back to the line.

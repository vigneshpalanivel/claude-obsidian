## 1b. 🔴 How the InnBlockchain funding was actually routed — stated 2026-09-24

**Not a bank-to-bank inter-company loan.** As described:
1. **Trioangle paid salary to the directors**; that amount was then **sent on to InnBlockchain**.
2. **People working for InnBlockchain were legally employed by Trioangle** and paid as Trioangle employees.

So the ₹1Cr+ "investment" is substantially **(a) director remuneration used as a conduit and (b) Trioangle carrying InnBlockchain's payroll** — not a documented loan.

> **Vignesh's reading: "legally Bowshul just has the shares; there is no Trioangle–InnBlockchain legal relationship."**
> **That reading is the wrong way round. The absence of documentation is exposure, not protection.**

**Why it cuts against Vignesh, not for him:**

| | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **The ₹46.7L may not be an enforceable debt** | Helps — Bowshul cannot easily sue InnBlockchain on it. **But the ₹60L "repaid" then has no documented basis either**, so the strongest argument in [[bowshul-issues-log]] (57% serviced) largely evaporates. Cannot claim credit for repaying a debt that does not exist on paper. |
| **₹1Cr+ of Trioangle's money applied to benefit another company, undocumented, common directorship** | **This is a worse fact pattern than a documented loan, for the director who arranged it.** A loan with a board resolution is defensible. An undocumented application of company funds is the classic basis for a **s.166 breach of director duty** claim and for an **oppression / mismanagement petition under s.241–242**. Bowshul holds 50% of the lender — he has standing. |
| **Trioangle expensing salaries for staff who worked for InnBlockchain** | Expenses claimed by the wrong entity. Income-tax exposure for Trioangle; InnBlockchain's P&L overstated because its true payroll cost sat elsewhere — **which also means InnBlockchain's +₹9L / +₹18.45L profitability is flattered and is not a like-for-like comparison with Trioangle's losses.** |
| **PF / ESI registered under Trioangle for InnBlockchain workers** | Potential misstatement to EPFO / ESIC. |
| **Staff effectively supplied between related parties without invoicing** | Under **Schedule I of the CGST Act, supplies between related parties are taxable even without consideration.** Unbilled inter-company services are a GST exposure. |
| **Related-party disclosure** | s.188 Companies Act, AS-18 / Ind AS 24. If the substance was related-party funding, the form does not save it — assessors apply substance over form. |
| **s.185 / s.186** | If substance is a loan to an entity in which a director is interested, the routing through salary does not cure it. |

**⚠ Note on framing:** informal commingling between commonly-owned SMEs is **very common in India and usually done for convenience, not to defraud anyone**. That is a mitigating context, not a defence. The exposure is real regardless of intent, and it sits with the people who signed the accounts.

**🔴 What this means for the separation:**
1. **Two companies with commingled payroll and undocumented funding cannot be cleanly separated by a share swap.** Unwinding requires reconstructing what belongs to whom — which means disclosure.
2. **It threatens the EU plan directly.** ECSP / MiCA-type authorisation runs fit-and-proper and governance checks, and any serious investor or acquirer doing diligence on InnBlockchain will find this. This is a bigger obstacle to the blockchain business than Bowshul is.
3. **It is a shared exposure, but not an equal one.** Bowshul signed as a director too — but Vignesh arranged the structure and drove both funded entities.
4. **Both partners are better off fixing this jointly than fighting about it.** This is the strongest argument for a cooperative separation that exists in these files — and it should be raised *with* him, not held as leverage.

**→ FIRST ACTION, ahead of everything else in these files: take the actual routing to a CA and a company secretary and ask what the exposure is and how to regularise it.** Do not open the separation conversation before that answer exists. Nothing here should be treated as a professional opinion.

- Stated verbatim: *"has some complications, internal book actual book, two accounts there."*

### 🔴 The parallel-records issue is the most serious item in any of these files

Two sets of accounts is a different category from informal commingling. Informal inter-company arrangements between commonly-owned SMEs are ordinary, fixable, and usually a matter of penalties and back-taxes. **Maintaining parallel records is not in that category.** The provisions that come into play — for a professional to assess, not me — include:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Companies Act s.128** | Books must give a true and fair view |
| **Companies Act s.447 / s.448** | Fraud; false statements. s.447 is **non-compoundable** and carries imprisonment |
| **Income Tax Act s.271AAD** | Penalty for false entries in books |
| **Income Tax Act s.276C / s.277** | Wilful attempt to evade tax; false statement in verification |
| **Audit** | If accounts were audited, the audit report is implicated too |

**Consequences that bear directly on the plan in [[bowshul-issues-log]]:**

1. **🔴 The EU plan cannot be built on this entity as it stands.** MiCA / ECSP authorisation runs fit-and-proper and integrity assessments on management. Parallel accounting is not a hurdle to clear — it is disqualifying if it surfaces. **The separation was conceived in order to build a regulated EU blockchain business. This is a larger obstacle to that than Bowshul has ever been.**
2. **Any diligence finds it.** Investor, acquirer, banking partner, auditor, licensing authority. The question is when, not whether.
3. **The ₹46L is unusable in either direction.** Vignesh cannot claim repayment credit for it; Bowshul cannot enforce it. **And it must not be written into any deal document** — putting an internal-book figure into a share transfer agreement or settlement deed creates a signed record of the parallel accounting.
4. **Both directors are exposed.** Bowshul signed as a director for the same periods. This is shared, which is precisely why it is better solved together than fought over.
5. **⚠ Do not attempt to tidy the records.** Retrospective alteration is materially worse than the original position. Whatever is done must be done by a professional, on advice, through whatever voluntary-regularisation route actually applies.

The plan assumes InnBlockchain is the vehicle for the EU regulated business. If it cannot pass a fit-and-proper / integrity review, the options are: **regularise InnBlockchain fully first**, or **use a clean new entity** — which reopens the corporate-opportunity problem in [[bowshul-issues-log]] §5 (taking InnBlockchain's work to a company Vignesh owns alone while still a 50% shareholder). **These are the same question, and it cannot be answered without the professional advice in step 1.**

### 1d. 🔴 Why the current CA is the wrong first call — 2026-09-24

**My assumption:** the existing CA can advise on this and will not tell Bowshul until I have spoken to him.

**Three reasons that does not hold:**

**1. He may be the person who maintained the parallel records.**
If so he has his own exposure, and **cannot advise objectively on remediating it** — his incentive is to minimise, delay, or steer toward whatever protects him. This is the decisive objection: **never take advice on the records from whoever kept them.**

**2. He is the companies' CA, not mine — and Bowshul is a 50% shareholder and a director of both.**
His duty runs to the companies. A director has statutory rights of access to the company's books (Companies Act s.128). **There is no confidentiality from a co-director over the company's own affairs**, so asking him to withhold puts him in a conflict I have created. And **if he is the statutory auditor, s.143(12) obliges him to report suspected fraud** above the threshold to the board / Central Government — telling him may trigger an obligation rather than secure a confidence.

**3. A CA carries no legal privilege in India.**
Privilege under s.126 of the Evidence Act attaches to legal practitioners, not chartered accountants. A CA can be compelled to produce records and testify. **"Confidential" here is a courtesy, not a protection.**

**⚠ And the timing logic is backwards.**
If the CA tells Bowshul before I do, I lose control of the disclosure *and* look as though I was concealing it — which is worse than the underlying facts. **The risk of the CA disclosing argues for raising it with Bowshul sooner under my own framing, not for a longer period of holding it.**

**→ Correct first call: an independent advocate — tax or corporate counsel — who carries privilege. Counsel then engages an independent or forensic CA. Keep the incumbent CA out of the initial assessment precisely because he may be implicated.** He can be brought in later, on counsel's instruction, for the remediation mechanics.

*Nothing in this section is a legal or accounting opinion. It is a flag, written to make sure the right questions reach the right professional.*

First positive P&L in four years (+₹2L), but revenue run-rate has collapsed: ₹58L over ~48% of the year annualises to roughly **₹1.2Cr vs ₹2.1Cr last year** — down ~43%. Its total profit across all four periods is ₹18.45L, so ₹1Cr+ was never repayable out of earnings on any sensible horizon. Combined with the fact that it was never documented (§1b), **calling it a loan was the error; removing it from the asset side corrects the books toward what actually happened** — Trioangle capitalised InnBlockchain and got shares in nothing for it. QoreUps has had **no assigned staff since ~March 2026**, existing clients paying **server cost only**, and no revival criteria ([[bowshul-issues-log]] §2). **H. Governance and compliance exposure on the related-party lending.**

# Client Call Introduction — Vignesh (InnBlockchain)

Reusable spoken introduction for InnBlockchain client calls. Modular blocks, not a paragraph.

## ⚠️ Read This Before Using Any Script Below

**1. Your role here is usually *escalation*, not discovery.**
Per the Discovery Call Master Sequence, Vasanth (SP) runs the discovery call. You are the
escalation path — brought in on regulatory confusion, technical depth, or deal weight.
**Most of your InnBlockchain intros are joining a call already in motion.** That is a different
speech act from opening one, and it uses a different opener. Both are below.

**2. The Trioangle credential does not transfer cleanly.**
"800+ platforms since 2015" is a **Trioangle-group** figure. Use it as *enterprise backing*,
never as *blockchain track record*.
🔴 **Do not lead with it in Channel Partner conversations at all** — advisory firms hear scale
and think circumvention risk (they fear you'll go direct to their client).

**3. InnBlockchain wins on regulatory precision, not volume.**
A regulated EU FinTech buyer has heard "we do blockchain" from fifty vendors. What they have
almost never heard is a vendor who gets the MiFID II / MiCA classification boundary right,
unprompted, in the first thirty seconds. **That is your entire differentiator. Lead with it.**

**4. EU Continental only, until the 5-project gate clears.**
No UK, UAE, US, Singapore, MENA or SEA outbound. MiCA is the dominant framing.

## The Core Script — Opening a Call (30 seconds)

### Block A — Who you are (8 sec)

> "I'm Vignesh, founder of InnBlockchain.
> We're a blockchain engineering team — we build smart contracts, and we audit them before mainnet.
> We're the Web3 division of Trioangle, which I also founded. Eleven years, 800+ platforms delivered."

**The Trioangle line is doing one job:** telling a regulated buyer you are not a two-person crypto shop
that disappears. That's it. Say it once, then move on. Do not build the pitch on it.

### Block B — What you actually do now (8 sec)

> "Most of our work right now is real-world asset tokenization for EU issuers.
> ERC-3643, ONCHAINID, transfer restrictions enforced in the contract itself —
> not a token with a compliance label bolted on top."

**Specificity is the credential here.** Naming the standards correctly does more for you
than any client count would.

### Block C — The regulatory line (8 sec) ← your strongest 8 seconds

> "We build against MiCA, MiFID II, and the DLT Pilot Regime.
> And those are different regimes. If your instrument is a transferable security,
> MiCA is not the one you're under — MiFID II is.
> That single distinction changes the whole architecture."

**Why this works:** it tells the buyer you know something they may have wrong, without
telling them they're wrong. That is the correct posture for the CEO of a young firm —
authority through precision, not through history.

Deliver it slowly. This is the sentence they'll remember.

### Block D — Set the frame (6 sec)

> "I'm not pitching you today unless you ask me to.
> I've got thirty minutes and I mostly want to understand your roadmap."

Taken from your own Discovery Call Master Sequence, step 6. It removes the pressure from
both sides of the call — including yours.

### Block E — Hand it back (5 sec)

> "So — what are you building, and what classification are you working to?"

**This is the escape hatch.** It ends the speaking burden and moves you to listening.
The second half of the question is doing real qualifying work: if they can't answer it
cleanly, you've learned the most important thing on the call inside the first minute.

## Variant 1 — Escalation Entry (your most common case)

You are joining a call Vasanth already opened. **Do not re-introduce the company.**
Re-introducing what has already been covered is the clearest junior signal available.

> "Hi — I'm Vignesh, I founded InnBlockchain. I lead the engineering side.
> Vasanth asked me to join because you're working through the classification question,
> and that's usually where these projects go wrong early.
> He's briefed me, so I won't make you repeat anything.
> Can I ask two questions and then tell you what I'd do?"

**"I won't make you repeat anything"** — high-value line. It shows the internal handoff worked,
which is itself proof of an organized vendor.

**"Can I ask two questions and then tell you what I'd do"** — sets a container. You know exactly
how long you have to speak, which is the single best anxiety reducer available in a live call.

## Variant 2 — Crypto Native founder (Felix — asset-class-first)

Skip regulation as the opener. He wants to know you understand *his* asset class and chain.

> "I'm Vignesh, founder of InnBlockchain.
> We build and audit smart contracts — DeFi protocols, DEX infrastructure, tokenization, wallets.
> Before anything else: what are you tokenizing, and on which chain?
> Because the answer changes everything I'd say next."

## Variant 3 — FinTech CTO (Tom — technical-first)

He is evaluating whether you're a real engineer or a salesperson. Give him depth immediately.

> "I'm Vignesh — founder, and I'm on the engineering side, not the sales side.
> We do ERC-3643 permissioned token work: on-chain identity, transfer-restriction modules,
> forced transfers and recovery for lost keys, and the erasure problem when GDPR
> meets an immutable ledger.
> That last one is where most implementations are quietly non-compliant.
> Where are you in your build?"

**The GDPR-vs-immutability point is a genuine differentiator** — you have done the work
(cross-contract erasure coordinator, off-chain resolution). Most vendors have not thought about it.

## Variant 4 — FinTech compliance lead (Carol — compliance-first)

She does not care about your architecture. She cares whether you will fail her audit.

> "I'm Vignesh, founder of InnBlockchain.
> I'll skip the technology — you'll want to know how we affect your compliance position.
> We build to MiCA, MiFID II, the DLT Pilot Regime, and DORA.
> You get the audit report, the vendor risk pack, and the DORA Article 30 data sheet.
> One question before anything else — will this engagement be classified as supporting
> a critical or important function under your DORA framework?"

🔴 **That last question is the highest-leverage sentence in this entire file.**
Your own DORA Article 30 Vendor Readiness Pack notes that *almost no vendor asks it*.
Asking it moves you, in one sentence, from "blockchain vendor" to "vendor who has done this before."
It costs you nothing and you cannot fake your way into it by accident.

## Variant 5 — FinTech business buyer (Sam — outcome-first)

No standards, no regime names. Outcome and risk only.

> "I'm Vignesh, founder of InnBlockchain.
> Simply — we build the on-chain part of regulated financial products, and we make sure
> it passes an audit before it goes live.
> The two things that kill these projects are the wrong regulatory classification at the start,
> and code that was never independently reviewed.
> We handle both. What's the product you're trying to launch?"

🔴 **Suppress the 800+ figure entirely.** Scale reads as circumvention threat to a partner.

## Words to Delete — InnBlockchain Specific

The general register list in [[client-call-intro-trioangle]] applies here too. These are additional:

| ❌ Never say | Why | ✅ Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "We are MiCA compliant" | A vendor cannot be compliant — the *issuer* is. You'd be telling a compliance officer you don't understand her job. | "We build to MiCA requirements" |
| "Blockchain is the future" | Reads as 2021 crypto salesperson | *(delete)* |
| "We do everything blockchain" | Breadth destroys credibility with regulated buyers | Name three things you did last quarter |
| "It's 100% secure" | No auditor says this. Ever. | "It's audited, and here's the report" |
| "Trust me, we've done this before" | Promise, not proof | "Here's the contract address. Go look." |
| "800+ blockchain projects" | ❌ Factually wrong — that's the Trioangle group figure | "800+ platforms across the group since 2015" |
| "Immutable, so it's GDPR-safe" | Backwards, and a compliance lead will catch it | "Erasure resolves off-chain — here's how" |

## The Two Reflexes to Build

**1. "I don't know — I'll confirm and come back to you Thursday."**
In regulated finance this is worth more than a confident answer. A vendor who guesses about
a regime is a liability, and everyone on that call knows it.

**2. Slow down on the regime names.**
MiCA, MiFID II, DLT Pilot, DORA, ERC-3643 — these are the words that must land cleanly.
If any one of them is rushed or blurred, the credibility of the whole block goes with it.
Say each one slightly slower than the sentence around it.

## How to Practice

1. Record Blocks A–E. **Target 30–35 seconds.** Over 45 means you're padding.
2. Drill Block C alone until it's automatic. It's your strongest asset and it must not wobble.
3. Drill the DORA question (Variant 4) until you can say it without reading.
4. Practice Variant 1 (escalation entry) most — it's your most frequent real case.

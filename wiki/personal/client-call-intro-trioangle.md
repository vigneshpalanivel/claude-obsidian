# Client Call Introduction — Vignesh (Trioangle)

Reusable spoken introduction for client calls. Built as **modular blocks**, not a paragraph —
you will be interrupted ~15 seconds in, and a memorized paragraph collapses when that happens.

**Target length: 30 seconds.** Not 90. A long self-introduction is a junior signal.

**Design constraints applied:**
- Sentences under ~12 words — built to be *spoken*, not read
- No idioms that can be misused ("on your dime", "hit the ground running")
- Leads with AI, per [[Trioangle Service ICP]] service-line priority
- 800+ stat disambiguated proactively (cumulative since 2015, not active clients)
- Trust defended with structural guarantees, never with promises

Related: [[english-training]] · [[Trioangle]] · [[Vignesh]]

## The Core Script (30 seconds)

### Block A — Who you are (8 sec)

> "I'm Vignesh. I started Trioangle in 2015, so I've run it for eleven years now.
> We build the platforms behind on-demand and marketplace businesses.
> Ride-hailing, delivery, home services, rentals."

### Block B — The credential (8 sec)

> "Since 2015 we've delivered more than 800 platforms, across 150 countries.
> That's every platform since 2015 — not our current client list. I want to be accurate about that."

**Why volunteer the caveat:** proactively qualifying your own number is a senior move.
It costs you nothing and it buys credibility for every other claim you make on the call.
Do not drop this line to sound bigger.

### Block C — Why that matters (8 sec)

> "So when you describe a dispatch problem, or a commission flow, or an availability engine —
> I'm not learning it on your budget. We've already built it."

**Do NOT replace this with "we build faster with AI."** That invites *"then why are you expensive?"*
Domain depth is the moat. Speed is not.

### Block D — What we do now (8 sec)

> "Most of our work today is AI inside platforms that are already running.
> Dispatch, pricing, demand forecasting, support.
> Not AI strategy decks — features that ship."

### Block E — Hand the call back (5 sec) ← the most important block

> "But you didn't take this call to hear about us.
> Tell me what's happening on your side."

**This is the highest-value sentence in the script.** It ends your speaking burden at 30 seconds
and moves you into listening — where your anxiety is lowest and your actual advantage is highest.
You are strongest asking questions about a domain you have run for eleven years.
Get there fast.

## Variants by Call Type

### 1. Inbound — they requested the call

Use all five blocks, but **compress B and C**. They already read the website; repeating it wastes
your credibility window.

> "I'm Vignesh, I founded Trioangle — we've been building marketplace and on-demand platforms since 2015.
> You've seen the site, so I won't repeat it.
> I'd rather hear what made you reach out. What's the problem you're trying to solve?"

### 2. Outbound — you requested the call

They owe you nothing. **Earn the next 30 seconds in the first 10.**

> "Thanks for the time — I'll keep this short.
> I'm Vignesh, I founded Trioangle. We've built over 800 on-demand and marketplace platforms since 2015.
> I reached out because [specific observation about their platform].
> I may be wrong about that. Is it worth ten minutes?"

**"I may be wrong about that"** is deliberate. It signals you are not running a script,
and it makes disagreement easy — which is how you get real information.

### 3. AI-forward operator (Persona 5 — direct primary)

Lead with **Block D first**, then A, then C. They care about the AI capability, not the company history.

> "I'm Vignesh, founder of Trioangle.
> We embed AI into platforms that are already in production — dispatch, pricing, forecasting, moderation.
> The difference is we built the platforms first. Eleven years, 800+ of them.
> So we know your edge cases before we start.
> What's on your AI roadmap right now, and what's blocking it?"

### 4. Founder with platform pain (Personas 1–2 — direct secondary)

Lead with **Block C**. Their pain is the platform, not AI. Surface AI by the second meeting, not this one.

> "I'm Vignesh — I've run Trioangle since 2015.
> We've rebuilt a lot of platforms that were built cheap and fast, and then stopped scaling.
> That's most of what we do.
> Before I say anything else — what's breaking?"

### 5. Channel partner / agency (Personas 3–4)

You are the **supplier of capability**, not the vendor. Different register: peer-to-peer, not seller-to-buyer.

> "I'm Vignesh, founder of Trioangle. We're the engineering team behind on-demand
> and marketplace platforms — 800+ since 2015, 150 countries.
> We work under agency partners more often than direct.
> Your client never has to know our name.
> What does your delivery gap look like?"

## The 30 Seconds After the Intro

Your intro provokes one predictable objection. Every buyer in this ICP has been burned before.

**They will say some version of:** *"We've used offshore teams before and it didn't work out."*

**Never answer with promises.** "We're transparent," "we communicate well," "our team is senior" —
every vendor who failed them said exactly that. Verbal reassurance has zero conversion value.

**Answer with structural guarantees they can verify in writing:**

> "You should be skeptical. Everyone who let you down said the same things I could say now.
> So here's what's in the contract instead.
> You own the GitHub repo from day one — not at delivery, day one.
> If we go quiet for 48 hours, you get a 5% contract credit.
> And you meet the actual engineers before you sign anything.
> If any of that doesn't happen, you have a paper trail, not a promise."

## Words to Delete Permanently

These are grammatically perfect and all of them position you **below** the buyer.
Register, not grammar, is what tells a US operator whether you're a peer or a supplier.

| ❌ Never say | ✅ Say instead |
|---|---|
| "Sorry, my English is not so good" | *(nothing — never raise it)* |
| "We will try our best" | "We'll do it" / "That one I'd need to check" |
| "As per your convenience" | "What time works for you?" |
| "Kindly let me know" | "Let me know" |
| "Please do the needful" | "Can you send that over?" |
| "I will revert back" | "I'll get back to you Thursday" |
| "Hope this is fine" | *(delete — say nothing)* |
| "Please let me know if any concerns" | "What am I missing?" |
| "We are having 800+ clients" | "We've delivered 800+ platforms" |
| "Myself Vignesh" | "I'm Vignesh" |
| "Uber clone" / "Airbnb clone" | "white-label platform" / "production-ready platform" |
| "Same same" / "only" as emphasis | *(delete)* |

**"I don't know" is a senior phrase, not a weak one.** Use it:
> "I don't know. I'll find out and come back to you Thursday."
That sentence builds more trust than any confident guess.

## How to Practice This

1. Record yourself speaking all five blocks. Time it. **If it's over 40 seconds, you're adding filler.**
2. Practice Block E alone, 20 times. It has to be automatic — it's your escape hatch out of speaking.
3. Practice being interrupted: have someone cut you off in Block B, then continue at Block E.
   Never restart from Block A.
4. Speak slower than feels natural. Target 130–150 words per minute.

**Do not read this off the screen during a live call.** Reading changes your rhythm and it is audible.
Learn the blocks, then close the file.

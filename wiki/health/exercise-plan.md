# Exercise Plan — Vignesh

**Equipment:** EFX elliptical + 3.5kg dumbbells (pair)
> ⚠️ **3.5 kg will plateau in 2–3 weeks for most dumbbell exercises.** Upgrade to 8 kg minimum by end of Week 3 — non-negotiable for lean gain past month 1. Until then, push-up progressions are the primary hypertrophy driver.
> **Session order:** Warmup (3.5 min) → Workout A or B → Balance training (2 min). Balance goes last — it's not fatiguing and works as a cooldown.
**Goal:** Muscle gain for BMI 17.4 → Phase 1: 70kg (BMI 20.7, **the floor**) · Phase 2: 75kg (BMI 22.2, **the actual target**). See [[bmi-increase-plan]].
**Long-horizon purpose:** This is the execution layer for Pillars 1–3 of [[healthspan-80]] — muscle, aerobic fitness, and bone density for active independence at 80. Resistance training is a permanent fixture, not a phase.
**Constraint:** Seronegative spinal/SI joint inflammation — spine-safe only. See [[profile]].

> ⚠️ **Personal spine history (critical):** SI joint dysfunction triggered ~6 years ago by 10+ hour travel with wallet in back pocket (asymmetric pelvic pressure). Free squats (aiming for 50 reps) caused a relapse ~2 years ago after a long symptom-free period. **Free squats and any deep-hip-flexion loaded movement are permanently off the table without explicit ortho clearance.** The SI joint is a structural weak point — never push through lower back or sacral sensation during any exercise.

## Warmup — Mandatory Before Every Session (5 min)

> ⚠️ **Never skip.** Left shoulder has a 14-year chronic irritation — cold joints + unpositioned movement = pain flare. SI joint is also vulnerable without warmup. 5 min is non-negotiable.

| # | Exercise | Duration | Why |
|---|----------|----------|-----|
| 1 | Arm circles (forward + backward) | 30 sec each direction | Wakes up shoulder joint, lubricates rotator cuff |
| 2 | Shoulder rolls (forward + backward) | 30 sec each direction | Releases left shoulder tension before load |
| 3 | Neck rolls (slow, side to side) | 30 sec | Cervical decompression |
| 4 | Hip circles (hands on hips) | 30 sec each direction | Primes SI joint before any leg/glute work |
| 5 | Knee raises (marching on spot) | 1 min | Raises core temperature, activates hip flexors |

**Total: ~3.5 min. Do this before Workout A and Workout B every single session.**

## Phase 1 — Weeks 1–4 (Foundation, Spine-Safe)

**Focus:** Form and control. No rushing. All bodyweight or 3.5kg.

### Workout A

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Cue |
|---|----------|-------------|-----|
| 1 | **Glute bridge** | 3 × 15 | Squeeze glutes at top, hold 2 sec. Protects SI joint. |
| 2 | **Dead bug** | 3 × 10/side | Lower back flat on floor the entire time — if it arches, you've gone too far |
| 3 | **Floor push-up** | 3 × 15 | Full range, chest touches floor. Hands shoulder-width. **Primary progressive overload exercise — progress through variations below.** |
| 4 | **One-arm dumbbell row** (3.5kg, knee on chair) | 3 × 12/side | Back flat, don't rotate spine, pull elbow to hip |
| 5 | **Dumbbell bicep curl** (3.5kg) | 3 × 12 | Standing, controlled — no swinging at the shoulder |
| 6 | **Wall sit** | 3 × 30 sec | Back flat against wall, thighs parallel to floor — isometric quad work, zero spine movement. Build to 60 sec. |
| 7 | **Bodyweight squat** | 1 × 10 | Controlled, quarter-to-parallel depth only. Feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out. Stop immediately at any sacral or lower back sensation. Never push to fatigue. **Max 10 reps — volume is what caused the relapse, not the movement.** |
| 8 | **Calf raises** (bodyweight) | 3 × 20 | Slow up, slow down — against wall for balance |

### Workout B

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Cue |
|---|----------|-------------|-----|
| 1 | **Bird dog** | 3 × 10/side | Spine neutral, slow — opposite arm + leg, hold 3 sec |
| 2 | **Glute bridge** | 3 × 15 | Same as Workout A |
| 3 | **Seated dumbbell shoulder press** (3.5kg) | 3 × 12 | Seated = spine supported. Press straight up, don't arch lower back |
| 4 | **Seated rear delt fly** (3.5kg) | 3 × 12 | Lean forward from seated, elbows out to sides — builds upper back posture |
| 5 | **Clamshell** (bodyweight) | 3 × 15/side | Lie on side, knees bent 45°, feet together — lift top knee like a clamshell. Directly stabilises SI joint. |
| 6 | **Single-leg glute bridge** | 3 × 10/side | Same as glute bridge, one leg extended — more glute/hamstring load, zero spine stress |
| 7 | **Dumbbell tricep kickback** (3.5kg) | 3 × 12 | Hinge forward, upper arm parallel to floor, extend only the forearm |
| 8 | **Side-lying leg adduction** | 3 × 15/side | Lie on side, bottom leg raises straight up — opposite of clamshell. Inner thigh (adductor). Keep hips stacked, no rolling. |
| 9 | **Prone hip extension** | 3 × 12/side | Lie face down on mat, one leg lifts straight up off floor, hold 1 sec. No lower back arch — squeeze glute, not back. Targets hamstrings + glutes. |
| 10 | **Calf raises** (bodyweight) | 3 × 20 | — |

**Rest between sets:** 60–90 sec

## Balance Training — Daily Add-On (2 min)

> **Placement:** End of every strength session (Workout A and B), after calf raises. Also fine standalone on EFX or rest days — it's not fatiguing.

**Why it's in the plan:** Falls are the leading cause of injury death in over-65s and the single largest risk to world travel in old age — see [[healthspan-80]] Pillar 1. Balance is trainable and declines fast if untrained. Also relevant now: single-leg stability work doubles as hip/SI joint stabiliser training.

| # | Exercise | Duration | Cue |
|---|----------|----------|-----|
| 1 | **Single-leg stand** | 30 sec/side | Stand near a wall or doorframe — fingertips available but not touching. Hips level, don't let the standing hip drop. Look straight ahead at a fixed point. |
| 2 | **Tandem stance** (heel-to-toe) | 30 sec | One foot directly in front of the other, heel touching toe. Arms at sides. Like standing on a tightrope. Switch front foot halfway. |
| 3 | **Heel-to-toe walk** | 10 steps | Walk a straight line placing heel directly against the toe of the other foot each step. Slow. Hallway wall nearby for reassurance — don't lean on it. |

**Total: ~2 min.**

### Progression Ladder

| Level | When to advance | Change |
|-------|----------------|--------|
| 1 | Start here | All three with eyes open, wall nearby |
| 2 | Level 1 stable for 1 week | Single-leg stand → **eyes closed**, 15 sec/side, build to 30 |
| 3 | Level 2 stable | Tandem stance → **eyes closed**, 15 sec, build to 30 |
| 4 | Level 3 stable | Heel-to-toe walk → **turn head left/right** while walking |
| 5 | Level 4 stable | Single-leg stand on a **cushion or folded towel** (unstable surface), eyes open |

### Rules

- **Always have a wall or doorframe within arm's reach** — especially Levels 2+. Eyes-closed balance work has a real fall risk.
- **No bare feet on slippery floor** — bare feet on a mat or rough floor is ideal.
- **Stop if dizzy.** You have low BP episodes (90/60, 100/60) and a PICA vascular loop — dizziness is a stop signal, not something to push through. See [[profile]].
- Balance improves fast at first, then plateaus — don't expect linear progress.

### Tracking

Log to [[exercise-log]] every **6 months**, not daily.

| Metric | Now | Age 40 target | Age 80 floor |
|--------|-----|---------------|--------------|
| Single-leg stand, eyes open | baseline TBD | 60 sec | 10 sec |
| Single-leg stand, eyes closed | baseline TBD | 30 sec | — |

> Single-leg stand time is one of the better-validated proxies for fall risk and functional age. Record a baseline now at 34 so the decades-long trend is visible.

## Cognitive Training — Daily/Weekly Add-On

**Why it's in the plan:** see [[healthspan-80]] Pillar 4 — chronic sleep debt, years of unmedicated anxiety, and elevated baseline cortisol are real dementia risk factors, not hypothetical ones. Being honest about what actually moves the needle here, rather than padding the list:

| Lever | Evidence strength | Where it lives |
|-------|--------------------|-----------------|
| **Aerobic exercise** | Strongest single evidenced lever for brain volume/BDNF | Already Pillar 2 — gated behind weight gain to 65kg first, same constraint as everywhere else in this plan. Not duplicated here. |
| **Sleep** | Strongest daily lever — glymphatic clearance happens in deep sleep | Already the top line of [[anxiety-stress]] — not duplicated here. |
| **Dual-task training** (physical + cognitive load together) | Genuinely evidenced for both fall-prevention and cognitive reserve in aging research | **New — added below**, piggybacks on Balance Training since you're doing that anyway |
| **Novel motor skill acquisition** | Learning a genuinely new movement pattern drives neuroplasticity differently than repeating known exercises | **New — added below**, low time cost |
| Generic "brain games" (Sudoku, crossword, brain-training apps) | Weak evidence for transfer to real-world cognitive decline | **Deliberately not included** — would be padding, not a real lever |

### Dual-task add-on (piggybacks on Balance Training, +0 min)

Once single-leg stand and tandem stance (Level 1) are comfortable, add a mental task on top of the same 2-minute block — no extra time required:

| Exercise | Add this cognitive load |
|----------|--------------------------|
| Single-leg stand | Count backward from 100 by 7s (100, 93, 86...) |
| Tandem stance | Name a category out loud (fruits, cities, companies) without repeating |
| Heel-to-toe walk | Do simple math in your head (17 × 3, etc.) while walking the line |

If balance visibly degrades when the mental task is added, that's expected and useful — it's the whole point of the exercise, not a sign you're doing it wrong.

### Novel skill — quarterly, not daily

Once a quarter, deliberately pick up a physical skill you don't already have (a new sport, a musical instrument, dance, juggling — anything genuinely unfamiliar). The novelty is the point, not the skill itself — repeating a known exercise, however hard, doesn't drive the same neuroplastic response as learning something new. No time target; even a few sessions per quarter counts.

## Stress-Handling Exercises — Daily Add-On

**Why it's here and not just in [[anxiety-stress]]:** that file has the full daily cortisol protocol (timing, triggers, the stress→symptom chain). This section is the physical technique layer only — the actual "exercises," kept short so this plan stays the single place to look for anything physical.

| Technique | How | When |
|-----------|-----|------|
| **Physiological sigh** | Two inhales through the nose (short then a bit longer) back-to-back, then one long slow exhale through the mouth | Any moment of acute work tension — fastest known way to drop arousal in real time |
| **Box breathing** | Inhale 4 sec → hold 4 sec → exhale 4 sec → hold 4 sec, repeat 4–6 cycles | Daytime, between meetings — more discreet than 4-7-8 for office use |
| **Progressive muscle relaxation (PMR)** | Tense each muscle group hard for 5 sec, then release completely, working feet → legs → glutes → abs → hands → arms → shoulders → face. One pass, ~5 min. | Evening, as part of wind-down — pairs directly with the reverse-Kegel relaxation work already in the Sexual Health section below; same "learn to fully release tension" skill, applied to the whole body instead of just the pelvic floor |

> 4-7-8 breathing and guided meditation are already in [[anxiety-stress]]'s evening protocol — not repeated here. This section only adds what wasn't already written down: the physiological sigh, box breathing, and PMR.

**Why PMR specifically matters for you:** chronic sympathetic activation from unmedicated anxiety tends to hold muscles in a low-grade tense state without you noticing it — the same mechanism already documented as contributing to pelvic floor tension in the Sexual Health section. PMR is the whole-body version of the same skill.

## Push-Up Progression Ladder (Primary Overload — No Equipment)

> Push-ups are your best lean muscle lever while dumbbells are light. Progress through these in order — each step increases load on chest, shoulders, and triceps without needing heavier weights.

| Level | Variation | Target | Muscles |
|-------|-----------|--------|---------|
| 1 | Floor push-up (standard) | 3 × 20 | Chest, triceps, front shoulder |
| 2 | Diamond push-up | 3 × 15 | Inner chest + triceps emphasis |
| 3 | Wide push-up | 3 × 15 | Outer chest + shoulder |
| 4 | Decline push-up (feet on chair ~40cm) | 3 × 12 | Upper chest + front shoulder |
| 5 | Pike push-up (inverted V position) | 3 × 12 | Shoulders — closest to overhead press |
| 6 | Archer push-up (shift weight to one arm) | 3 × 8/side | Heavy unilateral load — near single-arm |

Move to the next level when current level feels easy for 3 sets. Run Levels 1–3 in parallel from Week 2.

## Phase 2 — Weeks 5–8 (Add Load)

Add these exercises into A and B. Phase 1 movements continue — drop the easier sets if session runs long.

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Cue |
|---|----------|-------------|-----|
| + | **Floor dumbbell chest press** (3.5kg each) | 3 × 12 | Lie on floor — safer than bench, eliminates hyperextension risk |
| + | **Step-up** (bodyweight, low step ~20cm) | 3 × 12/side | Unilateral quad work — spine-neutral, no deep hip flexion. Use stairs. |
| + | **Side-lying hip abduction** | 3 × 15/side | Lie on side, raise top leg — hip stabiliser, protects SI joint |
| + | **Push-up progression** | 3 × 20 | Advance through the ladder above (diamond, wide, decline) |

> ⚠️ **Goblet squat and Romanian deadlift removed from Phase 2** — both involve patterns (deep hip flexion / loaded hip hinge) that risk SI joint flare given the free squat relapse history. Moved to Phase 3 only, with Dr. Rajapandian K clearance required before attempting either.

**Progressive overload rule:**
1. Hit 20 reps × 3 sets comfortably → add a 4th set
2. 4 sets × 20 reps → reduce rest from 90 → 60 → 45 sec
3. When weight feels easy → upgrade dumbbells (see table below)
4. After upgrading: reset to 3 × 10, build back to 3 × 15, then 3 × 20

| Current | Upgrade trigger | Next weight | Why |
|---------|----------------|-------------|-----|
| **3.5 kg** | Row, curl, press feel easy for 3 × 15 — likely **Week 2–3** | **8 kg** | Skip 5 kg — too small a jump for your frame; 8 kg gives real stimulus |
| **8 kg** | 3 × 15 feels easy across all exercises — roughly **Month 3–4** | **10–12 kg** | Standard progression for upper body compound work |
| **10–12 kg** | — | Consult Dr. Rajapandian K | Heavier loads = more spinal stress; need ortho clearance before going heavier |

## ⛔ Hard Stops — Never Do With Spinal Inflammation

- Barbell squats / conventional deadlifts (heavy axial spine load)
- Standing overhead press with heavy weight
- Twisting under load: Russian twists, loaded side bends, wood chops
- Sit-ups / crunches (spinal flexion under load)
- Anything that produces SI joint or lower back pain during or after — stop, rest 2 days, then retry at lower intensity

### Pelvic Floor Training — PE Management

> **Purpose:** PE management. Strongest evidence of any PE intervention — 60–80% of men see significant improvement at 4–6 weeks of daily consistency. Do this every single day, independent of the A/B strength schedule.

**How to find the muscle:** The muscle that stops urine mid-flow is the one you're targeting (PC muscle). Don't actually stop urine mid-flow as a practice — just use it once to locate it, then do the exercises dry.

**Three types, not one — a plain sustained hold alone is incomplete:**

| Type | What it trains | Why it matters here |
|------|-----------------|---------------------|
| **1. Slow holds** | Endurance/tone (slow-twitch fibers) | Foundation — builds general pelvic floor strength |
| **2. Quick flicks** | Reflexive control (fast-twitch fibers) | **Closer to the actual PE mechanism** — ejaculatory control at the point of urgency is a fast reflexive contraction, not a sustained hold. Slow holds alone don't train this. |
| **3. Reverse Kegels (relaxation)** | Ability to fully release the muscle | A pelvic floor that can't relax can't contract fully either. You already have a documented mechanism for chronic low-grade pelvic tension: sympathetic overdrive from unmedicated anxiety ([[anxiety-stress]]) keeps muscles subtly braced without you noticing. This directly applies. |

**1. Slow holds**

| Phase | Hold | Reps | Sets | When |
|-------|------|------|------|------|
| Week 1–2 | 3 sec squeeze → 3 sec release | 10 | 3 | Morning / lunch / evening |
| Week 3–4 | 5 sec squeeze → 5 sec release | 10 | 3 | Same |
| Week 5+ | 8–10 sec squeeze → 5 sec release | 10 | 3 | Same |

**2. Quick flicks** — add once you can reliably isolate the muscle (~week 2+)

- Squeeze and release as fast as possible, no hold at the top
- 15–20 rapid flicks, rest 15 sec, repeat for 3 rounds
- Once daily, any time — pairs well right after a slow-hold session

**3. Reverse Kegels (relaxation)** — add from week 1, evenings

- Instead of squeezing, consciously let the pelvic floor go slack — the opposite motion of a Kegel, like gently bearing down without straining
- 5 slow breaths, fully releasing on each exhale
- Do once in the evening — pairs naturally with the existing 4-7-8 breathing in [[anxiety-stress]]'s wind-down protocol

**Key rules (all three types):**
- Don't hold your breath — breathe normally throughout
- Squeeze/release only the pelvic floor — not glutes, thighs, or abs
- 100% invisible — do at desk, lying in bed, anywhere; no equipment, no gym
- **Consistency beats intensity** — daily short sessions beat occasional long ones
- 4–6 weeks before noticeable effect; 8–12 weeks for full benefit

**Quick sessions (2 min each):**
- Morning: 10 slow holds × 3 sec while having breakfast
- Afternoon: 10 slow holds × 5 sec at desk (no one knows) + 15 quick flicks
- Pre-sleep: 10 slow holds × 8 sec + reverse Kegel relaxation set, lying in bed

> See [[anxiety-stress]] PE section for the full management approach (stop-start technique, parasympathetic breathing, Dr. Sabrin consultation on dapoxetine/SSRI if pelvic floor training alone isn't enough after 3 months).
